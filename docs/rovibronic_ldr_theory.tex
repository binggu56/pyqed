\title{Full quantum geometric strongly correlated rovibronic dynamics for triatomic molecules: Theory,
 Implementation, and applications}
 \author{Sha Mo}
\author{Bing Gu}
\date{\today}


\begin{abstract}
We present a full quantum  rotation-vibrational-electronic (rovibronic) dynamics for triatomic molecules within the framework of the geometric quantum dynamics, that allows us to study correlated  strongly correlated vibronic motion beyond factorized approxmation. 
The rotational invariance of the potential energy surface and rotational covariance of the many-electron wavefunctions are fully exploited. 

THe critical components are the construction of the nuclear kinetic energy operators in internal curvilinear coordinates,
 and the construction of the global electronic overlap matrix in the body-fixed frame using the Eckart conditions. 
 
 For the electronic structure computations, we implement the complete active space scf method, also for the electronic overlap matrix construction.


The implementation is based on the  the discrete variable representation (DVR) and the geometric quantum dynamics. 

A detailed application is presented for the LiH$_2$ molecule to demonstrate the effectiveness of the method. 
 Implementation, and applications to  LiH$_2$
used for full quantum rovibronic wavepacket dynamics.  Starting from the
lab-frame molecular wavefunction, we separate internal coordinates from
overall molecular rotation, transform the electronic basis to a body-fixed
frame, derive the resulting nuclear kinetic energy operator, and obtain the
LDR equation of motion in a fixed total angular momentum basis
 We also state
the approximation made in the current implementation: body-fixed scalar
electronic adiabatic states are used, so explicit electronic angular
momentum gauge terms are neglected.
\end{abstract}

\section{Coordinate Transformation of the Nuclear Kinetic Energy}

The nuclear Cartesian coordinates can be written as
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{R}_A
  =
  \mathbf{R}_{\rm cm}
  +
  \mathbf{R}(\Omega)\,
  \mathbf{r}_A(q),
  \label{eq:coordinate-map}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{R}_{\rm cm}$ is the center of mass, $\mathbf{R}(\Omega)$ is
the rotation matrix from body to lab frame, and $\mathbf{r}_A(q)$ is the
position of atom $A$ in the body-fixed frame.

After removing translation, the Cartesian Laplacian becomes the
Laplace-Beltrami operator on the curvilinear coordinate space $(q,\Omega)$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{T}_{\rm nuc}
  =
  -\frac{1}{2}
  |g|^{-1/2}
  \partial_\mu
  \left[
    |g|^{1/2}
    G^{\mu\nu}(q)
    \partial_\nu
  \right],
  \label{eq:laplace-beltrami}
\end{equation}
where $G=g^{-1}$ is the inverse metric and $\mu,\nu = 1, 2, \ldots, 3N-3$ run over internal and
rotational coordinates.

In a body-fixed or Eckart frame, the inverse metric has block structure
\cite{eckart1935,watson1968,bunkerjensen1998,lauvergnat2016}
\begin{equation}
  G
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    G_{qq} & G_{q r} \\
    G_{r q} & G_{r r}
  \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $q$ labels vibrational coordinates and $r$ labels body-fixed
rotations.  To pass from the Podolsky form to an operator expression in
body-fixed angular momentum, one rewrites the Euler-angle momenta
\begin{equation}
  \hat{p}_\alpha = -i\pdv{}{\alpha},
  \qquad
  \hat{p}_\beta = -i\pdv{}{\beta},
  \qquad
  \hat{p}_\gamma = -i\pdv{}{\gamma},
\end{equation}
in terms of the generators of right rotations on the Euler-angle manifold.
For the standard $zyz$ Euler angles
$\Omega=(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$, one convenient body-fixed convention is
\begin{align}
  \hat{J}_x
  &=
  -\sin\gamma\,\hat{p}_\beta
  +
  \frac{\cos\gamma}{\sin\beta}
  \left(
    \hat{p}_\alpha - \cos\beta\,\hat{p}_\gamma
  \right),
  \nonumber\\
  \hat{J}_y
  &=
  \cos\gamma\,\hat{p}_\beta
  +
  \frac{\sin\gamma}{\sin\beta}
  \left(
    \hat{p}_\alpha - \cos\beta\,\hat{p}_\gamma
  \right),
  \nonumber\\
  \hat{J}_z
  &=
  \hat{p}_\gamma .
  \label{eq:J-from-pOmega}
\end{align}
Equivalently,
\begin{align}
  \hat{p}_\beta
  &=
  -\sin\gamma\,\hat{J}_x
  +
  \cos\gamma\,\hat{J}_y,
  \nonumber\\
  \hat{p}_\alpha
  &=
  \sin\beta
  \left(
    \cos\gamma\,\hat{J}_x
    +
    \sin\gamma\,\hat{J}_y
  \right)
  +
  \cos\beta\,\hat{J}_z,
  \nonumber\\
  \hat{p}_\gamma
  &=
  \hat{J}_z .
  \label{eq:pOmega-from-J}
\end{align}
The passage from the Podolsky operator to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:t-vib}--
\eqref{eq:t-cor} requires one intermediate bookkeeping step.  Define
\begin{equation*}
  \rho(q,\Omega) = |g(q,\Omega)|^{1/2},
  \qquad
  \hat{P}_{\mu} = -i\partial_{\mu}.
\end{equation*}
Then Eq.~\eqref{eq:laplace-beltrami} may be written as
\begin{equation*}
  \hat{T}_{\rm P}
  =
  \frac{1}{2}
  \rho^{-1}
  \sum_{\mu\nu}
  \hat{P}_{\mu}
  \rho G^{\mu\nu}
  \hat{P}_{\nu}.
\end{equation*}
This is still the raw Podolsky operator acting in the inner product with
measure $\rho\,dq\,d\Omega$.  For the DVR/LDR representation we instead
use the flat-measure wavefunction
\begin{equation*}
  \widetilde{\Psi}
  =
  \rho^{1/2}\Psi
  =
  |g|^{1/4}\Psi ,
\end{equation*}
so the operator acting on $\widetilde{\Psi}$ is
\begin{equation*}
  \widetilde{T}_{\rm P}
  =
  \rho^{1/2}\hat{T}_{\rm P}\rho^{-1/2}.
\end{equation*}
Substituting the Podolsky form gives
\begin{equation*}
  \widetilde{T}_{\rm P}
  =
  \frac{1}{2}
  \rho^{-1/2}
  \sum_{\mu\nu}
  \hat{P}_{\mu}
  \rho G^{\mu\nu}
  \hat{P}_{\nu}
  \rho^{-1/2}.
\end{equation*}
Now let this operator act on a flat-measure wavefunction $F$.  The right
momentum first acts as
\begin{equation*}
  \hat{P}_{\nu}
  \left(
    \rho^{-1/2}F
  \right)
  =
  \rho^{-1/2}\hat{P}_{\nu}F
  +
  \left(
    \hat{P}_{\nu}\rho^{-1/2}
  \right)F .
\end{equation*}
The terms containing derivatives of $F$ combine into a Hermitian quadratic
operator in the flat measure.  The terms in which the momenta act only on
the Jacobian factor $\rho^{-1/2}$ contain no derivatives of $F$ and define
the scalar metric pseudopotential,
\begin{equation*}
  V_{\rm extra}
  =
  \frac{1}{2}
  \rho^{-1/2}
  \sum_{\mu\nu}
  \hat{P}_{\mu}
  \left(
    \rho G^{\mu\nu}
    \hat{P}_{\nu}\rho^{-1/2}
  \right).
\end{equation*}
Equivalently, in derivative notation,
\begin{equation*}
  V_{\rm extra}
  =
  -
  \frac{1}{2}
  \rho^{-1/2}
  \partial_{\mu}
  \left[
    \rho G^{\mu\nu}
    \partial_{\nu}\rho^{-1/2}
  \right].
\end{equation*}
This gives the decomposition
\begin{equation*}
  \widetilde{T}_{\rm P}
  =
  \hat{T}_{\rm deriv}
  +
  V_{\rm extra}(q).
\end{equation*}
This equation is the definition of $\hat{T}_{\rm deriv}$ used below.  The
remaining Hermitian quadratic part is
\begin{equation*}
  \hat{T}_{\rm deriv}
  =
  \frac{1}{2}
  \sum_{\mu\nu}
  \hat{P}_{\mu}G^{\mu\nu}(q)\hat{P}_{\nu},
  \qquad
  \hat{P}_{\mu}
  =
  \left(
    \hat{p}_i,\hat{p}_{\Omega_a}
  \right),
\end{equation*}
with all terms generated only by derivatives of the Jacobian collected in
$V_{\rm extra}$.  Splitting the index
$\mu=(i,a)$, where $i$ labels internal coordinates and $a$ labels Euler
angles, gives
\begin{align*}
  \hat{T}_{\rm deriv}
  &=
  \frac{1}{2}
  \sum_{ij}
  \hat{p}_iG_{ij}\hat{p}_j
  +
  \frac{1}{2}
  \sum_{ab}
  \hat{p}_{\Omega_a}G_{ab}\hat{p}_{\Omega_b}
  \\
  &\quad+
  \frac{1}{2}
  \sum_{ia}
  \left[
    \hat{p}_iG_{ia}\hat{p}_{\Omega_a}
    +
    \hat{p}_{\Omega_a}G_{ai}\hat{p}_i
  \right].
\end{align*}
The rotational momenta are then changed from the Euler-angle basis to the
body-fixed generator basis.  Writing this linear transformation as
\begin{equation*}
  \hat{p}_{\Omega_a}
  =
  \sum_{\alpha}
  C_{a\alpha}(\Omega)\hat{J}_{\alpha},
\end{equation*}
where the explicit $C_{a\alpha}$ are those in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pOmega-from-J}, the rotational metric coefficients are
transformed as
\begin{align*}
  G_{\alpha\beta}(q)
  &=
  \sum_{ab}
  C_{a\alpha}G_{ab}C_{b\beta},
  \\
  G_{i\alpha}(q)
  &=
  \sum_a
  G_{ia}C_{a\alpha},
  \qquad
  G_{\alpha i}(q)
  =
  \sum_a
  C_{a\alpha}G_{ai}.
\end{align*}
The second block therefore becomes
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{1}{2}
  \sum_{ab}
  \hat{p}_{\Omega_a}G_{ab}\hat{p}_{\Omega_b}
  =
  \frac{1}{2}
  \sum_{\alpha\beta}
  G_{\alpha\beta}(q)
  \hat{J}_{\alpha}\hat{J}_{\beta},
\end{equation*}
while the mixed block becomes
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{1}{2}
  \sum_{i\alpha}
  \left[
    \hat{p}_iG_{i\alpha}(q)
    +
    G_{\alpha i}(q)\hat{p}_i
  \right]
  \hat{J}_{\alpha}.
\end{equation*}
Thus the $qq$ block is the vibrational term, the $rr$ block is the
rotational term, and the $qr+rq$ block is the Coriolis term.  After this
change of basis in the rotational tangent space, the
rovibrational kinetic energy takes the form
\begin{equation}
  \hat{T}_{\rm rv}
  =
  \hat{T}_{\rm vib}
  +
  \hat{T}_{\rm rot}
  +
  \hat{T}_{\rm cor}
  +
  V_{\rm extra}(q).
  \label{eq:t-rovib}
\end{equation}

The terms are
\begin{equation}
  \hat{T}_{\rm vib}
  =
  \frac{1}{2}
  \sum_{ij}
  \hat{p}_i
  G_{ij}(q)
  \hat{p}_j,
  \label{eq:t-vib}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \hat{T}_{\rm rot}
  =
  \frac{1}{2}
  \sum_{\alpha\beta}
  G_{\alpha\beta}(q)
  \hat{J}_\alpha
  \hat{J}_\beta,
  \label{eq:t-rot}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  \hat{T}_{\rm cor}
  =
  \frac{1}{2}
  \sum_{i\alpha}
  \left[
    \hat{p}_i G_{i\alpha}(q)
    +
    G_{\alpha i}(q) \hat{p}_i
  \right]
  \hat{J}_\alpha.
  \label{eq:t-cor}
\end{equation}
Here $\hat{p}_i=-i\partial/\partial q_i$, and
$\hat{J}_\alpha$ are body-fixed angular momentum operators.  The scalar
$V_{\rm extra}(q)$ is the curvilinear metric or pseudopotential term caused
by the volume element and operator ordering \cite{watson1968,lauvergnat2016}.

It is important to distinguish two equivalent normalization conventions.
Equation~\eqref{eq:laplace-beltrami} is the raw Podolsky/Laplace-Beltrami
form, which acts on a scalar wavefunction normalized with the curvilinear
volume element,
\begin{equation}
  \int dq\, d\Omega\, |g(q,\Omega)|^{1/2}\,
  |\Psi(q,\Omega)|^2
  =
  1.
\end{equation}
By contrast, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:t-rovib}--\eqref{eq:t-cor} are understood in the
equivalent Jacobian-regularized representation, obtained schematically by
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{\Psi}(q,\Omega)
  =
  |g(q,\Omega)|^{1/4}\Psi(q,\Omega).
\end{equation}
In that representation the norm is flat,
\begin{equation}
  \int dq\, d\Omega\, |\widetilde{\Psi}(q,\Omega)|^2 = 1,
\end{equation}
and the geometric effect of the Jacobian is carried by
$V_{\rm extra}(q)$.  Therefore, in the DVR/LDR implementation below, one
does \emph{not} insert an additional Jacobian factor in the coefficient
normalization.  The same logic applies to the rotational part: after
expansion in the orthonormal rotor basis $\ket{JKM}$, the Euler-angle
measure is already absorbed into the basis normalization rather than being
added again at the coefficient level.

\section{Lab-Frame Molecular Wavefunction}

Let the nuclear configuration of an $N$-atom molecule be described by
lab-frame Cartesian coordinates $\{\mathbf{R}_A\}$ and masses $\{M_A\}$.
The exact field-free molecular Coulomb Hamiltonian, before any body-frame
transformation, is
\begin{equation}
	\hat{H}
	=
	\hat{T}_\text{N}
	+
	\hat{H}_{\rm el}(\{\mathbf{R}_A\}),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	\hat{T}_{\rm N}
	=
	-\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_A \frac{1}{M_A}
	\nabla^2_{A}.
\end{equation}

In the lab frame (LF), the total rovibronic wavefunction can be expanded as a Born-Huang-like ansatz
\cite{bornhuang1954,bunkerjensen1998,watson1968,zare1988}
\begin{equation}
	\ket{\Psi(t)}
	=
	\sum_a
	\chi_a(\mathbf{q},\Omega,t)
	\ket{\Phi_\alpha(\mathbf{q},\Omega)}
	\label{eq:lab-expansion}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{q}$ denotes internal coordinates, $\alpha$ labels the electronic state, and
$
\Omega = (\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
$
denotes Euler angles specifying the orientation of the molecular body frame
relative to the lab frame.

For a field-free molecule, the electronic Hamiltonian is rotationally
covariant:
\begin{equation}
	\hat{H}_{\rm el}(q,\Omega)
	=
	\hat{U}_{\rm el}(\Omega)
	\hat{H}_{\rm el}(q,0)
	\hat{U}_{\rm el}^\dagger(\Omega).
\end{equation}
Thus the electronic eigenstates at orientation $\Omega$ are rotated copies
of body-fixed electronic states:
\begin{equation}
	\ket{\Phi_a(q,\Omega)}
	=
	\hat{U}_{\rm el}(\Omega)
	\ket{\phi_a(q)} ,
	\label{eq:electronic-rotation}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
	\hat{H}_{\rm el}(q,0)\ket{\phi_a(q)}
	=
	V_a(q)\ket{\phi_a(q)} .
\end{equation}
where $V_a(q)$ is the a-th adiabatic potential energy surface (APES), which only depends on the internal shape coordiantes. 

The body-fixed electronic representation is obtained by applying the orientation-dependent unitary transformation
\begin{equation}
	\ket{\Psi_{\rm body}}
	=
	\hat{U}_{\rm el}^\dagger(\Omega) \ket{\Psi_{\rm lab}} .
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:electronic-rotation},
\begin{equation}
	\ket{\Psi_{\rm BF}}
	=
	\sum_a
	\chi_a(q,\Omega,t)
	\ket{\phi_a(q)} .
\end{equation}
The explicit orientation dependence of the electronic basis has therefore
been removed.  It is now carried by the nuclear rotational wavefunction.

The rotational dependence of $\chi_a$ is expanded in Wigner rotational
functions \cite{zare1988,varshalovich1988}:
\begin{equation}
	\chi_a(q,\Omega,t)
	=
	\sum_{J K M}
	C_{J K M,a}(\mathbf q,t)
	D^J_{M K}(\Omega).
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
	\ket{\Psi_{\rm BF}(t)}
	=
	\sum_{J K M a}
	C_{J K M,a}(q,t)
	\ket{q}
	\ket{J K M}
	\ket{\phi_a(q)} .
	\label{eq:body-expansion-continuous}
\end{equation}
Here $J$ is total molecular angular momentum, $M$ is the projection onto the
lab-fixed $Z$ axis, and $K$ is the projection onto the body-fixed $z$ axis.

\section{Frame Transformation on the KEO}

The transformation
\begin{equation}
  \hat{H}_{\rm body}
  =
  \hat{U}_{\rm el}^\dagger(\Omega)
  \hat{H}_{\rm lab}
  \hat{U}_{\rm el}(\Omega)
\end{equation}
does not leave the nuclear kinetic energy unchanged, because
$\hat{T}_{\rm nuc}$ contains derivatives with respect to nuclear coordinates,
including derivatives with respect to $\Omega$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{U}_{\rm el}^\dagger
  \partial_\Omega
  \hat{U}_{\rm el}
  \neq
  \partial_\Omega .
\end{equation}
More generally, if $Q_\mu$ denotes any nuclear coordinate, either an
internal coordinate $q_i$ or an Euler angle, then the conjugated momentum
operator becomes
\begin{equation}
  \hat{U}_{\rm el}^\dagger
  \left(
    -i\partial_\mu
  \right)
  \hat{U}_{\rm el}
  =
  -i\partial_\mu
  +
  \hat{\mathcal A}_\mu,
  \qquad
  \hat{\mathcal A}_\mu
  =
  -i\hat{U}_{\rm el}^\dagger
  (\partial_\mu \hat{U}_{\rm el}) .
  \label{eq:covariant-momentum}
\end{equation}
Thus the frame change promotes ordinary derivatives to covariant
derivatives:
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\Omega
  \rightarrow
  \partial_\Omega
  +
  \hat{U}_{\rm el}^\dagger
  (\partial_\Omega \hat{U}_{\rm el}) .
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the full curvilinear KEO may be viewed as undergoing the
minimal-coupling substitution
\begin{equation}
  \hat{P}_\mu
  =
  \hat{P}_\mu + \hat{\mathcal A}_\mu,
\end{equation}
inside the metric form of the kinetic operator.

For derivatives with respect to the Euler angles, the connection is the
generator of electronic rotations:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{U}_{\rm el}^\dagger
  (\partial_\Omega \hat{U}_{\rm el})
  \sim
  -i \hat{\mathbf{j}}_{\rm el}.
\end{equation}
Therefore, in the most general body-fixed theory, the rotational angular
momentum entering the KEO is shifted schematically as
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{J}}_{\rm N} 
= 
  \hat{\mathbf{J}} - \hat{\mathbf{j}}_{\rm e}.
  \label{eq:j-shift}
\end{equation}
This is the body-fixed analogue of the gauge shift familiar from
Born-Huang theory, except that here the connection acts in rotational
space.  If the body frame itself depends on geometry, as in an
Eckart-embedded description, the meaning of the internal-coordinate
connection is easiest to see in the analytical triatomic KEO written in
bond-angle coordinates $q=(r_1,r_2,\theta)$.  The bare nuclear Eckart-frame
vibrational operator contains terms of the form
\begin{equation}
  \hat{T}_{\rm vib}^{\rm Eckart}
  =
  \frac{1}{2}
  \Big[
    G_{11}\hat{p}_{r_1}^2
    +
    G_{22}\hat{p}_{r_2}^2
    +
    G_{\theta\theta}\hat{p}_{\theta}^2
    +
    G_{12}\left(
      \hat{p}_{r_1}\hat{p}_{r_2}
      +
      \hat{p}_{r_2}\hat{p}_{r_1}
    \right)
    +
    G_{1\theta}\left(
      \hat{p}_{r_1}\hat{p}_{\theta}
      +
      \hat{p}_{\theta}\hat{p}_{r_1}
    \right)
    +
    G_{2\theta}\left(
      \hat{p}_{r_2}\hat{p}_{\theta}
      +
      \hat{p}_{\theta}\hat{p}_{r_2}
    \right)
  \Big]
  +
  V_{\rm metric}(r_1,r_2,\theta),
\end{equation}
with the coordinate-dependent coefficients
$G_{11},G_{22},G_{12},G_{1\theta},G_{2\theta},G_{\theta\theta}$ determined
by the Eckart-frame metric.  This operator already includes the geometric
effect of working in the Eckart frame through those $G$-matrix elements and
through the metric pseudopotential $V_{\rm metric}$.  No extra
``vector-potential'' term is needed for the \emph{purely nuclear} KEO.

The additional object
$\hat{\mathcal A}_i^{\rm el}$ appears only after introducing a
$q$-dependent electronic basis in the body frame.  In that case the
momentum operator acting on the rovibronic coefficient vector becomes a
covariant derivative,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{p}_{q_i}
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  \hat{p}_{q_i}\mathbf{1}_{\rm el}
  +
  \hat{\mathcal A}_i^{\rm el}(q),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
  \left(
    \mathcal A_i^{\rm el}
  \right)_{ab}
  =
  -i
  \braket{\phi_a(q)}{\partial_{q_i}\phi_b(q)} .
\end{equation}
Thus $\hat{\mathcal A}_i^{\rm el}$ is a matrix in electronic-state space;
it is not part of the bare Eckart nuclear KEO, and it is also distinct from
the later overlap matrix $A_{na,mb}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:electronic-overlap}.
In the most complete rovibronic theory, both
$\hat{\mathcal A}_i^{\rm el}$ and $\hat{\mathbf j}_{\rm el}$ enter the
transformed kinetic operator.

For example, the rotational contribution becomes
\begin{equation}
  \hat{T}_{\rm rot}
  \sim
  \frac{1}{2}
  \sum_{\alpha\beta}
  G_{\alpha\beta}(q)
  \left(
    \hat{J}_\alpha - \hat{j}^{\rm el}_\alpha
  \right)
  \left(
    \hat{J}_\beta - \hat{j}^{\rm el}_\beta
  \right).
\end{equation}
Likewise, the Coriolis part acquires the same covariant structure,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{T}_{\rm cor}
  \sim
  \frac{1}{2}
  \sum_{i\alpha}
  \left[
    \left(\hat{p}_i + \hat{\mathcal A}^{\rm el}_i\right) G_{i\alpha}(q)
    +
    G_{\alpha i}(q)\left(\hat{p}_i + \hat{\mathcal A}^{\rm el}_i\right)
  \right]
  \left(
    \hat{J}_\alpha - \hat{j}^{\rm el}_\alpha
  \right),
\end{equation}
while the pure vibrational operator is obtained from
$\hat{p}_i \rightarrow \hat{p}_i + \hat{\mathcal A}^{\rm el}_i$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:t-vib}.  These expressions show that the transformed KEO
contains not only the usual scalar pseudopotential but also matrix-valued
gauge couplings between adiabatic electronic channels.

The present implementation assumes scalar, body-fixed adiabatic electronic
states with quenched explicit electronic angular momentum, so
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{j}}_{\rm el} \approx 0.
\end{equation}
In addition, the body-fixed electronic states are assumed to be chosen in a
gauge where the internal-coordinate connection
$\hat{\mathcal A}^{\rm el}_i$ is
negligible or absorbed into the overlap-based LDR representation introduced
below.  Under these approximations,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:t-vib}--\eqref{eq:t-cor} are used with bare
$\hat{p}_i$ and $\hat{\mathbf{J}}$ only.

\subsection{Geometric quantum dynamics and  DVR Regularization}

Let the internal coordinates be represented by DVR grid points $q_n$.  The
body-fixed LDR expansion is built on the DVR idea that coordinate-local
grid functions provide an efficient representation of nuclear kinetic
operators and local potentials \cite{lightcarrington2000}.  The expansion is
\begin{equation}
  \ket{\Psi(t)}
  =
  \sum_{n \tau a}
  C_{n\tau a}(t)
  \ket{q_n}
  \ket{J\tau}
  \ket{\phi_a(q_n)} ,
  \label{eq:ldr-expansion}
\end{equation}
where
$ \tau = (K,M) $
for the full $|J K M\rangle$ basis, while
$
  \tau = K
$
for a fixed-$M$ block.

The local electronic overlap matrix is
\begin{equation}
  A_{n a, m b}
  =
  \braket{\phi_a(q_n)}{\phi_b(q_m)} .
  \label{eq:electronic-overlap}
\end{equation}
This overlap must be evaluated in a consistent body-fixed or Eckart gauge.
If electronic states are computed in inconsistent lab-frame orientations,
$A$ contains spurious rotational gauge dependence.

The electronic potential energy is local and diagonal:
\begin{equation}
  \bra{\phi_a(q_n)}
  \hat{H}_{\rm el}(q_n)
  \ket{\phi_b(q_n)}
  =
  E_a(q_n)\delta_{ab}.
\end{equation}

The rovibrational KEO matrix is
\begin{equation}
  T^J_{n\tau,m\tau'}
  =
  \mel{q_n,J\tau}
  {\hat{T}_{\rm rovib}}
  {q_m,J\tau'} .
  \label{eq:tj-matrix}
\end{equation}

Projecting the time-dependent Schrodinger equation gives
\begin{equation}
  i\dot{C}_{n\tau a}(t)
  =
  \left(V_a(q_n) + V_{\rm extra}(q_n)\right) C_{n\tau a}
  +
  \sum_{m\tau' b}
  T_{n\tau,m\tau'}
  A_{n a,mb}
  C_{m\tau' b}.
  \label{eq:ldr-eom-compact}
\end{equation}
This is the  LDR rovibronic equation of motion.

If one separates the derivative part of the KEO from the scalar
pseudopotential, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ldr-eom-compact} may be written more
explicitly as
\begin{align}
  i\dot{C}_{n\tau a}
  &=
  \left[
    E_a(q_n) + V_{\rm extra}(q_n)
  \right]
  C_{n\tau a}
  \nonumber\\
  &\quad+
  \sum_{m\tau' b}
  T^{J,{\rm deriv+rot+cor}}_{n\tau,m\tau'}
  A_{n a,m b}
  C_{m\tau' b}.
  \label{eq:ldr-eom-explicit}
\end{align}

\section{Special Cases}

\subsection{$J=0$}

For $J=0$, there is only one rotational state:
\begin{equation}
  K = M = 0,
  \qquad
  n_{\rm rot}=1.
\end{equation}
All angular momentum matrices vanish:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{J}_x = \hat{J}_y = \hat{J}_z = 0.
\end{equation}
Therefore,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{T}_{\rm rot}=0,
  \qquad
  \hat{T}_{\rm cor}=0,
\end{equation}
and the dynamics reduces to vibronic dynamics with the curvilinear
vibrational KEO:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{T}_{\rm nuc}
  =
  \hat{T}_{\rm vib}
  +
  V_{\rm extra}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Fixed $J_z=M$}

For field-free dynamics, the lab-frame projection $M$ is conserved.  One may
therefore work in a fixed-$M$ block:
\begin{equation}
  \ket{J K M_0},
  \qquad
  K=-J,\ldots,J.
\end{equation}
The rotational dimension is then
\begin{equation}
  n_{\rm rot} = 2J+1,
\end{equation}
instead of the full
\begin{equation}
  n_{\rm rot} = (2J+1)^2.
\end{equation}
This reduction is valid for field-free rovibronic dynamics.  In the presence
of external fields, whether $M$ remains conserved depends on the polarization
and selection rules of the light-matter interaction.

\section{Implementation details}

The current PyQED implementation uses the following assumptions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Electronic states are scalar body-fixed adiabatic states
  $\ket{\phi_a(q)}$.
  \item Electronic overlaps $A_{na,mb}$ are computed in a consistent
  body-fixed or Eckart orientation.
  \item Explicit electronic angular momentum gauge terms
  $\hat{\mathbf{j}}_{\rm el}$ are neglected.
  \item The full rovibrational KEO includes vibrational, rotational,
  Coriolis, and metric pseudopotential terms.
  \item Setting $J=0$ gives vibronic dynamics.  Setting $J>0$ gives
  rovibronic dynamics.  Supplying a fixed $J_z=M$ exploits conservation of
  lab-frame angular momentum projection in field-free dynamics.
\end{enumerate}
